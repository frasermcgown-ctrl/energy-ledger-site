Understanding carbon intensity

What is carbon intensity?

Carbon intensity is how many grams of CO₂ are emitted for each kilowatt hour of electricity used, in gCO₂/kWh. It falls when wind, solar and nuclear make up more of the mix, and rises when more gas is burned.

What do very low, low, moderate and high mean?

They are bands set by the Carbon Intensity API to make the number easier to read at a glance. The bands are fixed, so the same number always gets the same label.

When is electricity cleanest?

Usually overnight and on windy or sunny afternoons when demand is low. Shifting flexible use, such as EV charging, heat pumps or batteries, into the cleanest half hours cuts emissions.

Can I use these figures for carbon reporting?

Live figures are for operational decisions. Annual carbon reporting, such as Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting, normally uses the government's published greenhouse gas conversion factors instead.

Why Great Britain, not the UK?

The data covers the GB transmission system. Northern Ireland is part of the all-island electricity market with Ireland and is not included.
