Understanding UK gas storage

Where is Britain’s gas stored?

Rough, a depleted gas field off the Yorkshire coast, is the largest site. The others connected to the national grid, including Aldbrough, Hornsea, Hill Top Farm, Hole House Farm, Holford and Stublach, are smaller salt caverns that can fill and empty quickly.

Why does the UK have so little storage?

Britain long relied on North Sea production and flexible imports instead of large seasonal stocks. Rough stopped storing gas in 2017 and has only partly returned since, so the UK holds far less storage relative to demand than countries such as Germany or Italy.

What do withdrawals and injections tell you?

Sites withdraw when gas is needed or prices are high, usually on cold days, and inject when gas is cheap or plentiful. A large net withdrawal in mild weather can signal tight supply elsewhere.

How does storage affect prices?

With little storage, Britain leans on LNG and Norwegian pipeline gas to cover cold snaps. Low stocks or fast drawdowns can push the NBP price and the winter forward curve higher.
