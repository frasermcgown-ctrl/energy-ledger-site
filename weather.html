    Understanding the outlook

    Why does the weather move power prices?

    Wind and solar have almost no running cost, so on windy or sunny days they push more expensive gas-fired plants out of the market and wholesale prices tend to fall. On still, cold days gas sets the price more often, and day-ahead and evening-peak prices tend to firm.

    How are the ratings set?

    Each rating is a judgement from Met Office forecasts at coastal and inland sites near Britain's main wind and solar areas, checked against Elexon's national wind forecast where it covers the day. Coastal forecasts understate wind at turbine height, so treat them as a guide to the direction of output, not a megawatt figure.

    Who should use this?

    Businesses on flexible, index-linked or pass-through contracts feel day-to-day price swings most. Fixed-price customers are shielded in the short term, but a run of still weeks feeds into the forward prices their next contract is set from.
